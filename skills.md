# Architectural Poster Design System: Willi Kunz Columbia GSAPP Deconstruction

This document details the visual logic, spatial ratios, and layout behaviors observed directly in the Willi Kunz exhibition poster for the Columbia University Graduate School of Architecture and Planning Historic Preservation program[cite: 1].

---

## 1. Canvas Geometry and Core Division Units

The format uses a tall 1 to 2 vertical proportion, roughly 600px wide by 1230px tall in digital space[cite: 1, 2].

Rather than spreading copy evenly across the page, the width is split into eight equal columns[cite: 1, 2]. 
- The leftmost three columns form an open spine and negative margin, occupying the first 37.5 percent of the width[cite: 1].
- The remaining five columns contain all the primary visual and informational content, taking up the right 62.5 percent of the sheet[cite: 1].

The design intentionally bleeds to the raw trim of the paper[cite: 1]. Solid black registration tabs touch the top and bottom borders directly, while the body text stays well clear of the edges[cite: 1, 2].

---

## 2. The Primary Vertical Datum Axis

A strict plumb line runs vertically down the poster at the 37.5 percent mark, exactly three columns in from the left[cite: 1]. 

This single line anchors three separate elements:
- The left crop edge of the architectural photograph aligns flush to it[cite: 1, 2].
- The left edge of the horizontal headline block, Master of Science in Historic Preservation, snaps directly to it[cite: 1, 2].
- The start of the body text field below aligns directly along the same boundary[cite: 1, 2].

This shared axis establishes clear structure across the poster, separating the quiet margin corridor on the left from the dense technical material on the right[cite: 1].

---

## 3. Modular Text Grid

The informational section in the lower quadrant does not run across the full width[cite: 1, 2]. Instead, it uses an asymmetrical three-column schedule:
- The left margin area remains clear white space, carrying only a single ghost circle[cite: 1, 2].
- Column 1 contains the program overview and curriculum details, starting directly on the primary axis[cite: 1].
- Column 2 contains information on research centers, Avery Library, and faculty appointments[cite: 1].
- Column 3 covers admissions, deadlines, and practical contact details, leaving a small breathing margin before the right paper trim[cite: 1].

Both gutters between the three text blocks share an identical fixed spacing of roughly 20px[cite: 2]. The text finishes well above the bottom border, leaving an intentional band of quiet space before the black crop mark at the base[cite: 1, 2].

## 4. The Green Tint Plate, Terrace Step, and Spatial Layers

The single muted sage green background plane functions as an architectural drawing plate and drafting underlay[cite: 1, 2]:

The Stepped Terrace Motif
Behind the rotated vertical university title, the green tint plate does not meet the white background in a simple flat line[cite: 1]. It features an architectural, stair-like stepped terrace element that ascends along the left margin spine[cite: 1].
- Geometry: The perimeter breaks into rigid 90-degree stair treads and risers, creating geometric plateaus that step upward and outward[cite: 1].
- Function: This stair-like terrace directly cradles the offset blocks of the vertical title text, transitioning the open white space of the upper spine into the solid color mass below[cite: 1]. It acts as a structural foundation for the typographic rail while reinforcing architectural motifs of elevation and section drawings[cite: 1].

Planar Shadow Depth
Unlike flat Swiss typography, the photograph features a solid, unfeathered cast shadow offset down and to the left[cite: 1]. This creates distinct physical planes: the white canvas, the terraced sage underlay, the cast shadow, and the photo itself[cite: 1].

The Undulating Wave Transition
Below the photo, the green plane relaxes into a gentle wave that dips near the spine and rises across the datum line before terminating at the right edge[cite: 1, 2].

Binder Punch and Drafting Cutouts
- The left margin features two binder-punch circles[cite: 1, 2]. One is a clean cutout exposing the white page inside the green field, while the other is a printed sage circle floating on the plain white paper below it[cite: 1, 2]. These mimic architectural portfolio binder holes, nodding to the practical handling of archival sheets[cite: 1].
- Three clean white knockout lines cut horizontally through the right shoulder of the green shape, adding a subtle drafting-ruler touch[cite: 1, 2].

## 5. Typographic Hierarchy and Weight Relationships

The layout uses condensed and regular weights of a neutral neo-grotesque type family, structured around a clear scale hierarchy[cite: 1, 2]:

Level 1: The Rotated Institutional Title
The text reads Columbia University Graduate School of Architecture and Planning, rotated 90 degrees counter-clockwise[cite: 1, 2]. It is set in an extra-bold condensed weight at roughly four times the size of the body text[cite: 1, 2]. The lines are tracked tightly with solid, compact leading so ascenders and descenders nearly touch[cite: 1, 2]. The text block interacts directly with the stair-like terrace step beneath it[cite: 1].

Level 2: The Horizontal Headline
The text reads Master of Science in Historic Preservation[cite: 1, 2]. It is set bold, at roughly two and a half times the body size, aligned flush-left along the primary axis right below the photograph[cite: 1, 2].

Level 3: The Body Text
The three descriptive columns use a clean, regular weight for the narrative and bold subheads for contact info[cite: 1, 2]. The copy is set flush-left with an un-justified ragged right edge, spaced with generous leading to maintain legibility at small sizes[cite: 1, 2].

## 6. Registration Marks and Intentional Grid Breaks

The layout deliberately breaks outside its own rules along the outer boundaries to celebrate the printing process[cite: 1]:
- Two black notches hang directly off the top trim edge, separated by a thin hairline slit[cite: 1, 2].
- A narrow black registration bar hugs the top right edge[cite: 1].
- A solid black rectangular block sits flush against the bottom trim toward the left side[cite: 1, 2].

These marks intentionally break the quiet margin, recalling offset lithographic sheet trims, register marks, and technical blueprint edges[cite: 1].
